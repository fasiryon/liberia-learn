      body:"Today we review two fundamental parts of speech: nouns and pronouns. Understanding these building blocks of language will make you a better writer and speaker.\n\nA noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea. In our Liberian context, nouns are everywhere: 'Monrovia' (place), 'teacher' (person), 'cassava' (thing), 'freedom' (idea). Nouns can be common (general: city, river, school) or proper (specific: Monrovia, St. Paul River, Monrovia Central Academy). Proper nouns always begin with a capital letter.\n\nNouns can also be concrete (things you can see, touch, hear, smell, or taste) or abstract (ideas, feelings, or qualities). 'Desk,' 'drum,' and 'palm butter' are concrete nouns. 'Courage,' 'education,' and 'peace' are abstract nouns. Liberia's motto 'The Love of Liberty Brought Us Here' contains both abstract nouns (love, liberty) and a pronoun (us).\n\nA pronoun is a word that takes the place of a noun. Instead of saying 'Mary went to the market because Mary needed rice,' we say 'Mary went to the market because she needed rice.' 'She' is a pronoun replacing 'Mary.'\n\nPersonal pronouns include: I, me, you, he, him, she, her, it, we, us, they, them. Subject pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they) perform the action. Object pronouns (me, you, him, her, it, us, them) receive the action.\n\nPossessive pronouns show ownership: my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, its, our/ours, their/theirs. 'This is my notebook' uses a possessive pronoun.\n\nDemonstrative pronouns point to specific things: this, that, these, those. 'Those mangoes are ripe' — 'those' is a demonstrative pronoun.\n\nCommon mistakes to avoid: Do not confuse 'their' (possessive), 'there' (place), and 'they're' (they are). Also, make sure pronouns agree with the nouns they replace in number and gender. If the noun is singular and female (like 'Jenneh'), use 'she' and 'her,' not 'they.'\n\nMastering nouns and pronouns is the first step toward writing clear, effective sentences. Every great Liberian writer — from journalists to authors — builds their work on these foundations.",
