Even though we have phones and technology, many people in Liberia still use letters to communicate, especially in rural areas where phone access can be limited. A letter is a thoughtful way to connect with someone who is far away — a relative in Monrovia, a friend who moved to another county, or even a community leader you want to thank.\n\nA friendly letter has five parts. Let us learn each one.\n\nThe first part is the heading. The heading includes your address and the date. You write it in the top right corner of the page. For example:\n\nKakata Rural School\nKakata, Margibi County\nLiberia\nFebruary 18, 2026\n\nThe second part is the greeting, also called the salutation. This is how you say hello. You write it on the left side, under the heading. Always use a comma after the greeting. Examples: 'Dear Auntie Musu,' or 'Dear Friend Varney,' or 'Dear Teacher.'\n\nThe third part is the body. This is the main message of your letter. It is where you write what you want to say. The body can be one or more paragraphs. Start by asking how the person is doing — this is polite in Liberian culture. Then share your news or reason for writing. You might tell them about school, your family, or something exciting that happened in Kakata. End the body with a warm closing thought.\n\nThe fourth part is the closing. This is how you say goodbye. Write it on the right side, below the body. Use a comma after the closing. Examples: 'Your loving niece,' or 'Your friend,' or 'With respect,' or 'Sincerely.'\n\nThe fifth part is the signature. This is your name, written below the closing.\n\nHere is an example of a complete friendly letter:\n\nKakata, Margibi County\nFebruary 18, 2026\n\nDear Cousin Blessing,\n\nHow are you? I hope you and your family are well in Monrovia. I am fine here in Kakata. I wanted to tell you about something exciting at our school. Our teacher taught us about the plants and water sources in our environment. We even took a walk around the school to look at different trees and plants. I learned that the rubber trees near our school are important for Margibi County.\n\nI also wanted to tell you that our market day is coming soon. Ma will be selling her pepper and garden eggs. I wish you could visit us. Please greet your mother and father for me.\n\nYour loving cousin,\nFatima\n\nNotice how the letter is organized and friendly. It follows all five parts. The writer asks about the other person first, then shares her news, and ends warmly.\n\nWhen writing a letter, use neat handwriting so the reader can understand you easily. Be polite and respectful. Organize your thoughts before you start writing so your letter flows naturally from one idea to the next. Check your spelling and punctuation before you finish.",
