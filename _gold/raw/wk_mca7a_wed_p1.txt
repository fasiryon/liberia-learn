This is one of the most important fraction skills you will learn.\n\nYou cannot add 1/3 + 1/4 directly because thirds and fourths are different-sized pieces. It is like trying to add Liberian dollars and US dollars without converting — you need a common unit first.\n\nThe common unit for fractions is called the Least Common Denominator (LCD). The LCD is the smallest number that both denominators divide into evenly. For 1/3 + 1/4, we need the LCD of 3 and 4. Since 12 is the smallest number divisible by both 3 and 4, our LCD is 12.\n\nStep 1: Find the LCD. Step 2: Convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the LCD as the denominator. Step 3: Add the numerators. Step 4: Simplify if needed.\n\nLet us work through 1/3 + 1/4. LCD = 12. Convert: 1/3 = 4/12 (multiply top and bottom by 4). Convert: 1/4 = 3/12 (multiply top and bottom by 3). Now add: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. Done!\n\nHere is a real-life example: A tailor in Waterside Market uses 1/3 of a yard of cloth for a collar and 1/2 yard for sleeves. How much cloth total? LCD of 3 and 2 is 6. Convert: 1/3 = 2/6 and 1/2 = 3/6. Add: 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6 of a yard.\n\nAnother example: A farmer uses 2/5 of his land for rice and 1/3 for vegetables. How much land is used? LCD of 5 and 3 is 15. Convert: 2/5 = 6/15 and 1/3 = 5/15. Add: 6/15 + 5/15 = 11/15 of his land.\n\nFinding the LCD: List multiples of each denominator until you find the smallest common one. Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20... Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15... The LCD is 15.\n\nThis skill takes practice, but once you master it, you can add any two fractions.
