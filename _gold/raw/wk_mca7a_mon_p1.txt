In Liberia, we use fractions every day — when we share a cup of rice among family members, when market women in Waterside Market cut fabric into equal parts, or when we divide palm butter soup among bowls at dinner. The bottom number is the denominator — it tells us how many equal parts the whole is divided into.\n\nImagine you have one whole pineapple from the Duala Market. Together you have 3/4 and one piece remains.\n\nFractions can represent quantities less than one, equal to one, or greater than one. When the numerator equals the denominator (like 4/4), the fraction equals one whole. When the numerator is larger than the denominator (like 5/4), we call it an improper fraction — it is more than one whole.\n\nIn Liberian markets, traders often think in fractions. If a bag of charcoal costs 500 LRD and you only want half a bag, you pay 250 LRD — that is 1/2 of 500. If cassava bread is cut into 8 slices and you buy 3 slices, you are buying 3/8 of the whole bread.\n\nLet us practice identifying fractions around us.
