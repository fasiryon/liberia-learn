Coast and inland: compare places using evidence

Read, picture and question the landscape

Read the geography passage. Say what coast and inland mean before comparing them. Sketch sea, coast and inland land. Label your sketch as a simple model without a scale. A sketch helps explain a relationship; it does not locate an actual school. Reading Liberia's landscape. Liberia lies in West Africa beside the Atlantic Ocean. Coastal means near the sea. Inland means farther from the coast. Liberia has coastal lowlands, with hills and higher land farther inland. These broad descriptions do not give every place the same shape. A plain is a broad area of fairly level land. A plateau is higher land with a fairly level surface. A physical region describes landscape features. It is not the same as a county boundary. Read a map's key before using its symbols.

Model a careful comparison

A careful comparison names a difference and explains its evidence. Coastal describes closeness to the sea. Inland describes being farther from the coast. Neither word gives an exact height. A county is an administrative area; a physical description concerns landscape. Do not draw county borders from these words. How can we compare without claiming too much? Step 1: Name the feature: distance from the coast. Step 2: Compare: a coastal location is nearer the sea than an inland location. Step 3: Limit: this description alone does not give an exact height or every person's work.
