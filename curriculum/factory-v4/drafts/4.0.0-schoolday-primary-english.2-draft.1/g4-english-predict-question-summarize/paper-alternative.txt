Show the difference between a clue and a fact

A reader thinks: Massa moved the tin under a roof, so she might try sheltering the labels. The movement is a stated detail. Sheltering is a prediction because we have not read the result yet. A useful question is: Will the names stay readable after rain? Which statement is already known from the opening? Options: Massa carried a tin under the veranda.; Every label will stay clear all week.; The class has already shared the seedlings..

Look for the action the narrator explicitly describes.

Choose evidence and a useful question

A prediction should fit the difficulty in the story. A question helps when its answer would change or deepen your understanding. Select the details that explain why readable labels matter, then choose a question the opening leaves unresolved. Massa and the garden labels: opening. After rain, the names on the school garden labels were hard to read. If the names disappeared, visitors might not know which plants they were taking. Massa carried a clean tin under the veranda. Which two details explain why the class needs readable labels? Which question is still open and useful? Options: Will the labels stay clear after another shower?; Did Massa carry a tin?; What is Massa's favourite song?.

Look for the link between the unreadable names and the visitors' choices.

An unanswered question asks for information the opening has not supplied.

Read what happened next

Now read the ending. Compare it with your prediction before changing any words. A different outcome does not make the earlier prediction dishonest if it was reasonable from the opening. Good readers update their thinking. Massa and the garden labels: ending. Massa's class put one spare label on an open bench and another inside the tin under the veranda. Both labels used the same paper and ink. After a shower, the bench label was hard to read. The sheltered label was still clear. The class repeated the comparison after another shower and saw the same pattern. They kept spare labels in the sheltered tin and checked them before sharing the seedlings. They had not tested every kind of rain. If later checks showed a problem, they would change the plan.

Separate a supported result from a sweeping claim

Work independently with the complete story. A result from two comparisons supports a particular conclusion, but it does not tell us what would happen in every possible storm. Decide which interpretation and summary preserve what the text actually shows. Which conclusion is supported by the ending? Options: The tin will protect every label in every storm forever.; The open-bench label stayed clearer in both comparisons.; The sheltered label stayed clearer in the two comparisons described.. Which short account best connects the problem, response and result? Options: Massa sold all the plants after proving that no label could ever blur again.; Massa had a tin. There were seedlings. Friday was coming.; Rain blurred the garden labels. The class compared open and sheltered labels, then kept spare labels sheltered after two clearer results..

Write an evidence-based summary

Write for a classmate who has not read the story. Include the central problem, the class's response and the result. Use your own words. Keep one limit of the evidence in mind rather than promising that the idea will always work. Summarize the problem, response and result in 30 to 60 words.
