### Reading and Writing Numbers to 100,000 (`learn`)

Learner action: Read the explanation and the examples.

Every digit in a number has a place, and the place tells its value. From the right, the places are ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands and hundred thousands. In 47,305 the 4 is in the ten thousands place, so it is worth 40,000. The 7 is worth 7,000, the 3 is worth 300, the 0 means there are no tens, and the 5 is worth 5.

We read the number in two parts, split by the comma. First read the thousands group, then say "thousand", then read the rest: 47,305 is "forty-seven thousand, three hundred five". We never say "and" inside a whole number.

Expanded form shows the value of each digit: 47,305 = 40,000 + 7,000 + 300 + 5. A zero keeps a place empty. Without it, 47,305 would become 4,735, a very different number.

Numbers this large appear in daily life: the number of people in a district, the cost of a motorbike in Liberian dollars, or the number of bags of rice arriving at the Freeport. The number 100,000 is one hundred thousand, the first six-digit number.

### Work it out together (`classwork`)

Learner action: Do the classroom activity with your group.

1. Place-value chart: learners draw a chart (hundred thousands to ones) in their books and place paper digit cards in it for numbers the teacher calls, then read each number aloud.
2. Place-value strips: learners stack paper strips (for example 40,000, 7,000, 300 and 5) to build 47,305, then pull the strips apart to show its expanded form.
3. Pairs: one learner builds a five-digit number with cards in the chart, and the partner reads it aloud and writes it in words.
4. Card game: learners arrange five digit cards to make the largest and smallest possible numbers, then read them.
