### Ma Musu's cassava garden (`hook`)

Learner action: Think about the two questions Ma Musu needs to answer.

Ma Musu in Bong County has a rectangular garden 6 metres long and 4 metres wide (illustrative numbers).

She needs to know two things: how much fence goes **around** the garden, and how much ground there is **inside** to plant cassava.

- Accessibility: A rectangular garden is 6 metres long and 4 metres wide. How much fence goes around it, and how much ground is inside? · keyboard: Read the text, then Continue.

### Around or inside? (`around-inside`)

Learner action: Open each step to see the garden drawn on squared paper.

Draw the garden on squared paper: each square is 1 metre by 1 metre. Open each step.

1. **Draw the garden** — A rectangle 6 squares long and 4 squares wide.
2. **Walk around it** — The perimeter is the distance around: 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 metres of fence.
3. **Count the inside** — The area is the number of squares inside: 4 rows of 6 squares, 6 × 4 = 24 square metres.

- Hints: Perimeter: around. Area: inside.
- Media DIAGRAM (MEDIA_OPTIONAL): A 6 by 4 rectangle on a square grid, with the border highlighted for perimeter and the squares shaded for area. — alt: A rectangle 6 squares long and 4 squares wide; its border measures 20 metres and it covers 24 squares.
- Accessibility: A 6 by 4 metre garden: the fence around it is 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 metres; the ground inside is 6 × 4 = 24 square metres. · keyboard: Each step is a button; press Enter to open it.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Squared exercise-book paper is the core modality.

### A square school garden (`square-example`)

Learner action: Follow each step of the worked example.

A square school garden has sides of 5 metres.

Perimeter: a square has 4 equal sides, so 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 metres, or 4 × 5 = 20 metres.

Area: 5 rows of 5 squares, 5 × 5 = 25 square metres.

The square and Ma Musu's garden have the same perimeter but different areas.

- Accessibility: A 5 metre square: perimeter 4 × 5 = 20 metres, area 5 × 5 = 25 square metres. Same perimeter as the 6 by 4 garden, different area. · keyboard: Read the text, then Continue.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Text only.

### Key ideas (`review`)

Learner action: Read the key ideas.

Remember:

- Perimeter is the distance around: add all the sides.
- Area is the space inside: count the squares or multiply length by width.
- Two shapes can have the same perimeter and different areas.

- Accessibility: Review: perimeter adds the sides; area counts or multiplies squares; equal perimeters can enclose different areas. · keyboard: Read the list.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Text only.

## Misconceptions

- `swaps-area-perimeter` (obj9): Multiplies the sides when asked for the perimeter, or adds them when asked for the area. → Ask what is being measured: the fence goes around (add the sides); the planting covers the inside (count or multiply the squares).
- `counts-two-sides` (obj9): Adds only the two given side lengths for the perimeter. → Walk the shape: a rectangle has four sides, and opposite sides are equal.
