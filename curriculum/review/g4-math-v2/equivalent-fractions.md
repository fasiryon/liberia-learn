## Scenes in full

### Who got more cassava bread? (`hook`)

Learner action: Predict who got more before reading on.

Fatu cut her cassava bread into 2 equal pieces and ate 1. Boima cut the same size bread into 4 equal pieces and ate 2.

Boima says he ate more because 2 is more than 1. Is he right? Make a guess before you go on.

- Accessibility: Fatu ate 1 of 2 equal pieces; Boima ate 2 of 4 equal pieces of the same bread. Who ate more? · keyboard: Read the text, then Continue.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Text only.

### What you will be able to do (`goal`)

Learner action: Read the goal.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Show that two fractions name the same amount using strips or drawings.
- Write equivalent fractions by multiplying the top and bottom by the same number.

- Accessibility: Goal: show and write equivalent fractions, using strips first and then multiplication. · keyboard: Read the list, then Continue.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Text only.

### Fold a strip (`fold`)

Learner action: Fold a paper strip and shade it as you open each step.

Take a paper strip. Follow each step with your own strip, then open the next step.

1. **Fold in half** — Fold the strip once and shade one part: you have shaded 1/2.
2. **Fold again** — Fold it in half again without unfolding. Open it: there are 4 equal parts and 2 are shaded. 2/4 covers the same part as 1/2.
3. **Fold a third time** — Fold again: 8 equal parts, 4 shaded. 4/8 is still the same shaded part.

- Expected observation (reviewer only): Learners see the same shaded length named 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8.
- Hints: Keep the shading the same; only the folds change.
- Accessibility: Folding a strip in half, then again and again, the same shaded part is 1/2, then 2/4, then 4/8. · keyboard: Each step is a button; press Enter to open it in order.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Paper strips are the core modality; the fraction visualizer is optional.

### Line the strips up (`compare-strips`)

Learner action: Open each row of the strip picture and compare where the shading ends.

Picture three strips of the same length, one under the other. Open each row and look at where the shading stops.

1. **Row 1: 1/2** — One of two equal parts is shaded; the shading stops at the middle.
2. **Row 2: 2/4** — Two of four equal parts are shaded; the shading also stops at the middle.
3. **Row 3: 4/8** — Four of eight equal parts are shaded; again the shading stops at the middle. All three name the same amount: they are equivalent fractions.

- Accessibility: Three equal strips: 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 shaded. In each, the shading ends at the middle, so the fractions are equivalent. · keyboard: Each row is a button; press Enter to open it.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Described in text; learners can draw the strips.

### The multiplying rule (`rule`)

Learner action: Read the rule and the example.

Each fold doubled the number of parts and doubled the shaded parts. That is the rule: **multiply the top and the bottom by the same number** and the amount stays the same.

1/2 = (1 × 3)/(2 × 3) = 3/6.

Adding the same number does not work: 1/2 is not 2/3.

- Multiply top and bottom by the same number.
- Never add the same number to top and bottom.

- Accessibility: Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same number to get an equivalent fraction: 1/2 times 3 over 3 is 3/6. Adding does not work. · keyboard: Read the text, then Continue.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Text only.

### Quick check (`check`)

Learner action: Choose an answer for each question.

Choose an answer. Read the feedback, even when you are right.

- Which fraction is equivalent to 1/3? — options: 2/4 / 2/6 (expected) / 2/3 / 1/6
- Back to the bread: did Boima (2/4) eat more than Fatu (1/2)? — options: Yes, because 2 is more than 1 / No, they ate the same amount (expected) / No, Fatu ate more
- Hints: Multiply, do not add.
- Accessibility: Two questions: which fraction equals 1/3, and whether 2/4 is more than 1/2. · keyboard: Tab to each option and press Space; feedback is read aloud.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Feedback works offline.

### Make your own equivalent fractions (`practice`)

Learner action: Write two equivalent fractions for each fraction.

Write two fractions equivalent to each fraction. Show the multiplication you used.

- Write two fractions equivalent to 2/5. (min 6 chars)
- Write two fractions equivalent to 3/4. (min 6 chars)
- Hints: Try multiplying the top and bottom by 2, then by 3.
- Accessibility: Write two equivalent fractions for 2/5 and for 3/4 in the text boxes, showing the multiplication. · keyboard: Tab to each text box and type.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Answers are kept on this device.

### Explain it in your own words (`reflect`)

Learner action: Write a short explanation.

Write a short answer. There is no single right wording.

- Explain to Boima why 2/4 and 1/2 are the same amount. (min 20 chars)
- Accessibility: Write an explanation of why 2/4 and 1/2 are the same amount. · keyboard: Tab to the text box and type.
- Offline (FULL_OFFLINE): Kept on this device.

## Misconceptions

- `adds-same-number` (obj5): Adds the same number to the top and bottom, thinking 1/2 = 2/3. → Fold or draw both fractions: 2/3 covers more of the strip than 1/2. Multiplying both parts keeps the amount the same; adding does not.
- `bigger-numbers-bigger-fraction` (obj5): Thinks 4/8 is more than 1/2 because the numbers are bigger. → Line the strips up: 4/8 and 1/2 reach exactly the same point.
