### Explanation

Multiplication is repeated addition of equal groups. Four groups of 6 bottle caps is 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24, written 4 x 6 = 24. An array (rows and columns) shows the same thing: 4 rows of 6.

Multiplication has properties that make facts easier:
- Commutative (order) property: 4 x 6 = 6 x 4. Turning the array sideways does not change the total.
- Identity property of one: any number times 1 is itself (9 x 1 = 9).
- Zero property: any number times 0 is 0 (9 x 0 = 0). Nine empty groups hold nothing.
- Associative (grouping) property: (2 x 3) x 4 = 2 x (3 x 4) = 24.
- Distributive property: split a hard fact into easier ones. 7 x 8 = 7 x 5 + 7 x 3 = 35 + 21 = 56.

Knowing facts up to 10 x 10 quickly helps with every later topic: long multiplication, division, fractions and area.

### Objectives

- Recall multiplication facts up to 10 x 10.
- Identify and use the commutative, identity, zero, associative and distributive properties.

### Classwork

- Build arrays with bottle caps or stones for 3 x 5 and 5 x 3 and compare the totals.
- Fact families: learners write the four facts for 6, 7 and 42 (6 x 7, 7 x 6, 42 / 6, 42 / 7).
- Break-apart: split 8 x 7 into 8 x 5 + 8 x 2 using a drawn array.

### Practice

1. 7 x 9 = ? **Answer:** 63
2. Which property shows 5 x 8 = 8 x 5? **Answer:** Commutative (order) property
3. Use the distributive property: 6 x 12 = 6 x 10 + 6 x 2 = ? **Answer:** 72
4. What is 145 x 0? **Answer:** 0

### Homework

1. 8 x 6 = ? **Answer:** 48
2. Fill the blank: (3 x 2) x 5 = 3 x (2 x ___) **Answer:** 5
3. Write two multiplication facts for an array of 4 rows of 9. **Answer:** 4 x 9 = 36 and 9 x 4 = 36

### Quiz

1. What is 9 x 8? (72 / 63 / 81 / 64) **Answer:** 72
2. Which is an example of the identity property? (7 x 1 = 7 / 7 x 0 = 0 / 7 x 2 = 2 x 7 / 7 x 3 = 21) **Answer:** 7 x 1 = 7
3. Which equals 4 x 13? (4 x 10 + 4 x 3 / 4 x 10 + 3 / 4 + 13 / 4 x 10 x 3) **Answer:** 4 x 10 + 4 x 3

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

### Exit assessment

### Teacher notes

### Explanation

Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place to the left and puts a zero in the ones place: 7 x 10 = 70. Multiplying by 100 moves digits two places (7 x 100 = 700), and by 1,000 three places (7 x 1,000 = 7,000).

Use a basic fact and then count zeros. For 6 x 400, think 6 x 4 = 24, then 400 has two zeros, so 6 x 400 = 2,400. For 3 x 5,000, think 3 x 5 = 15, add three zeros: 15,000.

Be careful when the basic fact already ends in zero: 5 x 60 is 5 x 6 = 30 plus one zero, which is 300. There are two zeros in the answer, not one.

This skill helps with money in Liberian dollars: if a bag of charcoal costs L$500 (example price), then 4 bags cost 4 x 500 = L$2,000.

### Explanation

To multiply a larger number by a one-digit number, multiply each place, starting with the ones, and regroup when a product is 10 or more.

Example: 348 x 6.
- Ones: 8 x 6 = 48. Write 8, carry 4 tens.
- Tens: 4 x 6 = 24, plus the 4 carried = 28. Write 8, carry 2 hundreds.
- Hundreds: 3 x 6 = 18, plus 2 = 20. Write 20.
So 348 x 6 = 2,088.

You can also use expanded form (the distributive property): 348 x 6 = 300 x 6 + 40 x 6 + 8 x 6 = 1,800 + 240 + 48 = 2,088.

Estimate to check: 348 is about 350, and 350 x 6 = 2,100, so 2,088 is reasonable.

### Explanation

Division shares a number into equal groups. In 84 / 4 = 21, 84 is the dividend, 4 is the divisor and 21 is the quotient.

Long division works from the left, one place at a time: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.

Example: 752 / 4.
- Hundreds: 7 / 4 = 1, 1 x 4 = 4, 7 - 4 = 3. Bring down 5 to make 35.
- Tens: 35 / 4 = 8, 8 x 4 = 32, 35 - 32 = 3. Bring down 2 to make 32.
- Ones: 32 / 4 = 8, 8 x 4 = 32, 32 - 32 = 0.
So 752 / 4 = 188.

Sometimes there is a remainder: 53 / 5 = 10 remainder 3, because 10 x 5 = 50 and 3 is left over. The remainder must always be less than the divisor.

Check division by multiplying: quotient x divisor + remainder = dividend. 188 x 4 = 752.

### Objectives

- Divide 2-, 3- and 4-digit numbers by a 1-digit divisor using long division.
- Interpret remainders and check division by multiplication.

### Classwork

- Fair sharing: share 96 bottle caps among 4 groups, first by tens, then by ones.
- Board routine: learners chant divide, multiply, subtract, bring down while working a problem together.
- Check partners: one learner divides, the partner checks by multiplying.

### Explanation

Sometimes a step in long division cannot make a group. Then we write 0 in that place of the quotient. Leaving it out gives a wrong answer.

Example: 824 / 4.
- Hundreds: 8 / 4 = 2. 2 x 4 = 8, 8 - 8 = 0. Bring down 2.
- Tens: 2 / 4 = 0 (2 is smaller than 4). Write 0 in the tens place. 0 x 4 = 0, 2 - 0 = 2. Bring down 4 to make 24.
- Ones: 24 / 4 = 6.
So 824 / 4 = 206, not 26.

Check: 206 x 4 = 824. And 26 x 4 = 104, which shows that 26 is wrong.

Estimating also catches the mistake: 824 / 4 is about 800 / 4 = 200, so the answer must be in the hundreds.

### Objectives

- Use estimation to check the number of digits in a quotient.

### Explanation

To solve a word problem, read it carefully and ask: What do I know? What must I find? Is this sharing into equal groups, or finding how many groups?

Sharing: 96 pencils are shared equally among 8 learners. How many does each get? 96 / 8 = 12.

Grouping: 96 pencils are packed 8 to a box. How many boxes? 96 / 8 = 12 boxes.

The remainder must make sense in the story:
- 50 learners ride in taxis that carry 4 each. 50 / 4 = 12 remainder 2. We need 13 taxis, because the last 2 learners must also ride. Round the quotient up.
- L$50 buys pens at L$4 each (example price). 50 / 4 = 12 remainder 2. You can buy 12 pens, and L$2 is change. The remainder is left over.

Always write the answer as a sentence with units, and check it by multiplying.

### Objectives

- Solve sharing and grouping problems using division.
- Decide what a remainder means in a word problem.

### Classwork

- Sort the story: groups sort problem cards into sharing and grouping.
- Remainder decisions: for each problem, decide whether to round up, drop the remainder or report it.
- Learners write their own division story about their school or market.

### Practice

2. 75 learners travel in buses that carry 20 each. How many buses are needed? **Answer:** 4 buses
3. L$90 is shared equally among 4 children (example amount). How much does each get, and how much is left? **Answer:** L$22 each, L$2 left
4. A 144-page book is read at 9 pages a day. How many days does it take? **Answer:** 16 days
