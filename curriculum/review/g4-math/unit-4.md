### Explanation

To multiply numbers that end in zeros, multiply the non-zero parts, then add all the zeros from both numbers.

30 x 40: 3 x 4 = 12, and there are two zeros in total (one in 30, one in 40). So 30 x 40 = 1,200.

20 x 300: 2 x 3 = 6, three zeros in total. So 20 x 300 = 6,000.

50 x 80: 5 x 8 = 40, two zeros in total, giving 4,000. The answer has three zeros because 40 already ends in zero.

Why it works: 30 x 40 = 3 x 10 x 4 x 10 = (3 x 4) x (10 x 10) = 12 x 100.

This is the mental step behind long multiplication and estimation.

### Explanation

An estimate is a quick, close answer. It tells you whether an exact answer is reasonable.

To estimate a product, round each factor to its greatest place, then multiply mentally.

38 x 52: round 38 to 40 and 52 to 50. 40 x 50 = 2,000. The exact answer (1,976) is close.

412 x 27: round 412 to 400 and 27 to 30. 400 x 30 = 12,000.

If both factors are rounded up, the estimate is more than the exact answer. If both are rounded down, it is less.

Use estimates to check your work. If you calculate 38 x 52 = 19,760, the estimate of 2,000 shows the answer is ten times too big.

### Objectives

### Explanation

To multiply by a 2-digit number, split the multiplier into tens and ones, find two partial products, and add them.

Example: 46 x 23.
- Multiply by the ones: 46 x 3 = 138.
- Multiply by the tens: 46 x 20 = 920. (Write a 0 in the ones place first, then multiply by 2.)
- Add: 138 + 920 = 1,058.

The same method works for larger numbers: 312 x 14 = 312 x 4 + 312 x 10 = 1,248 + 3,120 = 4,368.

An area model shows why: a rectangle 46 by 23 splits into 40 x 20, 40 x 3, 6 x 20 and 6 x 3. The parts add to 800 + 120 + 120 + 18 = 1,058.

Always estimate first: 46 x 23 is about 50 x 20 = 1,000.

- Use partial products and an area model.

### Explanation

Long division by a 2-digit divisor uses the same steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Estimates help choose each digit.

Example: 864 / 24.
- 86 / 24: think 80 / 20 = 4, but 4 x 24 = 96 is too big. Try 3: 3 x 24 = 72. 86 - 72 = 14. Bring down 4 to make 144.

So 864 / 24 = 36.

If a trial digit makes a product that is too big, try one less. If the remainder is bigger than the divisor, try one more.

Check: 36 x 24 = 864.

Making a short multiples list for the divisor (24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 144 ...) makes each step faster.

### Explanation

A decimal shows parts of a whole using place value. The decimal point separates whole numbers from parts.

To the right of the point: the first place is tenths (one part of 10 equal parts), the second is hundredths (one part of 100 equal parts).
0.1 = 1/10, 0.01 = 1/100, 0.35 = 35/100.

In 4.27, 4 is ones, 2 is tenths (0.2) and 7 is hundredths (0.07). Read it as "four and twenty-seven hundredths" or "four point two seven".

A hundred grid shows hundredths: shade 35 of 100 small squares to show 0.35.

Money uses hundredths: in US dollars, used alongside Liberian dollars, $2.75 is 2 dollars and 75 cents, and 75 cents is 75/100 of a dollar.

A zero holds an empty place: 0.05 is five hundredths, but 0.5 is five tenths (fifty hundredths).

### Objectives

- Read and write decimals to hundredths in words, numerals and fractions.
- Identify the value of each digit in a decimal.

### Classwork

- Money link: write amounts like 3 dollars 45 cents as 3.45.

### Explanation

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and compare place by place from the left.

3.47 and 3.52: the ones are equal (3 and 3). In the tenths, 4 < 5, so 3.47 < 3.52.

When the decimals have different numbers of places, add a zero so both have hundredths: 0.6 = 0.60. Then 0.60 > 0.58, so 0.6 > 0.58.

A longer decimal is not always bigger: 0.9 > 0.45, because 9 tenths is more than 4 tenths.

On a number line from 0 to 1 marked in tenths, 0.45 sits halfway between 0.4 and 0.5, and 0.9 is further right.

To order decimals, compare them in pairs or write them all with the same number of places, then order them like whole numbers: 0.30, 0.03, 0.33 become 0.03 < 0.30 < 0.33.

### Objectives

- Compare decimals to hundredths using >, < and =.
- Order decimals from least to greatest and greatest to least.

### Classwork

- Number line 0 to 1: learners place 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 0.05.
- Race times (example data): order the finishing times 12.45 s, 12.4 s and 12.54 s from fastest to slowest.
- Grid comparison: shade 0.6 and 0.58 on hundred grids to see which is bigger.

### Practice

2. Write >, < or =: 2.30 ___ 2.3 **Answer:** =
3. Order from least to greatest: 0.5, 0.05, 0.55 **Answer:** 0.05, 0.5, 0.55
4. Which is greater: 4.08 or 4.8? **Answer:** 4.8

### Homework

### Quiz

2. Which statement is true? (0.4 > 0.35 / 0.4 < 0.35 / 0.4 = 0.35 / 0.35 > 0.4) **Answer:** 0.4 > 0.35
