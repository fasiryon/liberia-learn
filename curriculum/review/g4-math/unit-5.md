### Explanation

Time is measured in seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months and years.
60 seconds = 1 minute, 60 minutes = 1 hour, 24 hours = 1 day, 7 days = 1 week.

To estimate time, compare with something familiar (a benchmark):
- Clapping your hands once takes about 1 second.
- Singing the national anthem takes about 1 to 2 minutes.
- A school lesson takes about 45 minutes.
- A night's sleep takes about 8 to 10 hours.

Choose the right unit: brushing teeth takes minutes, not hours. Travelling from Monrovia to Gbarnga by car takes hours, not minutes.

Good estimators check themselves. Estimate how long a task takes, time it with a clock or phone, then compare. With practice, estimates get closer.

### Objectives

- Choose a sensible unit of time for an activity.
- Estimate how long activities take using benchmarks.
- Compare estimates with measured times.

### Classwork

- Estimate then time: learners estimate how long it takes to write their name 10 times, then the teacher times it.
- One-minute challenge: learners close their eyes and stand when they think one minute has passed.
- Sort cards: activities sorted into seconds, minutes, hours and days.

### Practice

1. Which unit would you use to measure the time to eat lunch? **Answer:** Minutes
2. About how long is a school day: 7 minutes, 7 hours or 7 days? **Answer:** 7 hours
3. How many minutes are in 2 hours? **Answer:** 120 minutes
4. How many seconds are in 3 minutes? **Answer:** 180 seconds

### Homework

1. Estimate how long it takes you to walk to school. Then ask an adult to time it. Write both times. **Answer:** Answers vary; the estimate and measured time are both recorded
2. How many hours are in 2 days? **Answer:** 48 hours
3. Which is longer: 90 seconds or 1 minute? **Answer:** 90 seconds

### Quiz

1. About how long does it take to tie a shoe? (10 seconds / 10 minutes / 10 hours / 10 days) **Answer:** 10 seconds
2. How many minutes are in 1 1/2 hours? (90 / 60 / 150 / 100) **Answer:** 90
3. Which activity takes about 1 hour? (Walking about 5 kilometres / Blinking / Growing a mango tree / Saying your name) **Answer:** Walking about 5 kilometres

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

How many minutes are in one hour? (60 / 100 / 24 / 30) **Answer:** 60

### Exit assessment

Which is the best estimate for the time to wash a plate? (30 seconds / 30 minutes / 3 hours / 3 days) **Answer:** 30 seconds

### Teacher notes

This is a practical lesson. Record each learner's estimate and measured time as teacher-observation evidence.

### Materials

### Offline behavior

Fully offline: the teacher times tasks with a wall clock or phone. Practical; no device is needed by learners.

### Explanation

Elapsed time is how much time passes between a start time and an end time.

Count on in easy jumps: first to the next full hour, then whole hours, then the leftover minutes.

Example: school starts at 8:15 a.m. and break is at 10:40 a.m.
- 8:15 to 9:00 is 45 minutes.
- 9:00 to 10:00 is 1 hour.
- 10:00 to 10:40 is 40 minutes.
Total: 1 hour + 45 + 40 minutes = 1 hour 85 minutes = 2 hours 25 minutes.

A time line helps: draw a line, mark the start and end, and draw the jumps above it.

Finding an end time: a football match starts at 3:30 p.m. and lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. 3:30 + 1 hour = 4:30. 4:30 + 30 minutes = 5:00. 5:00 + 15 minutes = 5:15 p.m.

Crossing noon: from 11:20 a.m. to 1:05 p.m. is 40 minutes to noon, then 1 hour 5 minutes, making 1 hour 45 minutes.

### Objectives

- Find elapsed time between two times.
- Find an end time or start time given the elapsed time.
- Use a time line to count on.

### Classwork

- Paper-plate clocks: learners move the hands from the start time to the end time, counting hours and minutes.
- Class time line: find the elapsed time between events in the school day.
- Bus schedule (example times): find journey times between towns.

### Practice

1. From 9:00 a.m. to 11:30 a.m. is how long? **Answer:** 2 hours 30 minutes
2. From 7:45 a.m. to 8:20 a.m. is how long? **Answer:** 35 minutes
3. A lesson starts at 1:10 p.m. and lasts 45 minutes. When does it end? **Answer:** 1:55 p.m.
4. From 10:50 a.m. to 1:15 p.m. is how long? **Answer:** 2 hours 25 minutes

### Homework

1. From 6:30 a.m. to 7:15 a.m. is how long? **Answer:** 45 minutes
2. A market opens at 7:00 a.m. and closes at 6:00 p.m. How long is it open? **Answer:** 11 hours
3. A bus leaves at 2:40 p.m. and the trip takes 2 hours 35 minutes. When does it arrive? **Answer:** 5:15 p.m.

### Quiz

1. How long is it from 8:20 a.m. to 9:05 a.m.? (45 minutes / 85 minutes / 1 hour 15 minutes / 35 minutes) **Answer:** 45 minutes
2. A film starts at 4:15 p.m. and lasts 1 hour 50 minutes. When does it end? (6:05 p.m. / 5:65 p.m. / 5:05 p.m. / 6:15 p.m.) **Answer:** 6:05 p.m.
3. How long is it from 11:30 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.? (2 hours 30 minutes / 3 hours 30 minutes / 9 hours 30 minutes / 1 hour 30 minutes) **Answer:** 2 hours 30 minutes

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

What time is 30 minutes after 2:15? (2:45 / 2:30 / 3:15 / 2:50) **Answer:** 2:45

### Exit assessment

A class trip leaves at 9:40 a.m. and returns at 12:15 p.m. How long is the trip? (2 hours 35 minutes / 3 hours 25 minutes / 2 hours 25 minutes / 3 hours 35 minutes) **Answer:** 2 hours 35 minutes

### Teacher notes

Learners often subtract times like whole numbers (12:15 - 9:40 = 2:75). Use time lines so they count on in hours and minutes. No Grade 4 clock tool exists online yet; use paper-plate clocks.

### Materials

Paper plates and split pins (clocks), Wall clock

### Offline behavior

Fully offline: paper-plate clocks and drawn time lines. Online manipulative is an open gap (no clock tool for Grades 4-6).

Duration: 45 minutes.

| Example-data labels | none |

**Known uncertainties:** none recorded.

### Explanation

To measure length accurately with a ruler:
1. Line up one end of the object with the 0 mark, not the edge of the ruler.
2. Keep the object straight along the ruler.
3. Read the mark at the other end.

Rulers show inches with smaller marks for halves and quarters. A length that ends at the long mark between 3 and 4 is 3 1/2 inches.

For longer objects, use a yardstick or measuring tape. When the object is longer than the ruler, mark where the ruler ends, move it along, and add the lengths.

Write the unit with every measurement: 5 in, 3 ft, 2 yd. A number without a unit does not tell us the length.

Tailors in Liberia measure cloth in yards and inches, and carpenters measure timber in feet and inches.

### Objectives

- Measure lengths to the nearest inch, half inch and quarter inch.
- Measure longer lengths in feet and yards.
- Record measurements with units.

### Classwork

- Measure five classroom objects to the nearest half inch and record in a table.
- Measure the classroom length in feet using a tape, working in pairs.
- Error spotting: find what went wrong when a ruler starts at its edge instead of 0.

### Practice

1. A pencil reaches the 6 1/2 mark on a ruler starting at 0. How long is it? **Answer:** 6 1/2 inches
2. A crayon starts at the 1 mark and ends at the 4 mark. How long is it? **Answer:** 3 inches
3. A table is 1 yard and 1 foot long. How many feet is that? **Answer:** 4 feet
4. A ribbon measures 30 inches. Is it more or less than 1 yard? **Answer:** Less (1 yard = 36 inches)

### Homework

1. Measure three objects at home to the nearest inch and record them with units. **Answer:** Answers vary; each measurement includes a unit
2. A board is 5 feet long. How many inches is that? **Answer:** 60 inches
3. A cloth is 2 yards long. How many feet is that? **Answer:** 6 feet

### Quiz

1. A nail starts at 0 and ends halfway between 2 and 3. How long is it? (2 1/2 inches / 2 inches / 3 inches / 2 1/4 inches) **Answer:** 2 1/2 inches
2. Where should the end of an object be placed to measure it? (At the 0 mark / At the edge of the ruler / At the 1 mark / Anywhere) **Answer:** At the 0 mark
3. A stick is 48 inches. How many feet is that? (4 / 3 / 6 / 12) **Answer:** 4

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

How many inches are in 1 foot? (12 / 10 / 3 / 36) **Answer:** 12

### Exit assessment

An object starts at the 2 mark and ends at the 7 1/2 mark. How long is it? (5 1/2 inches / 7 1/2 inches / 9 1/2 inches / 5 inches) **Answer:** 5 1/2 inches

### Teacher notes

Use blunt-ended rulers only. Check learner measurements as teacher-observation evidence. Online, the digital-ruler tool can supplement but not replace real measuring.

### Materials

Rulers, Yardstick or tape, Objects to measure

### Offline behavior

**Known uncertainties:** 
- The lesson treats weight and mass together, using everyday customary units (ounce, pound). Confirm terminology.

### Explanation

Weight (mass) tells how heavy something is. Customary units are ounces (oz) and pounds (lb): 16 ounces = 1 pound.
Benchmarks: a slice of bread weighs about 1 ounce, a cup of rice about 7 ounces, and a large tin of tomato paste about 2 pounds. Rice is often sold in 25-pound and 50-pound bags.

Capacity tells how much a container holds. Customary units are cups, pints (pt), quarts (qt) and gallons (gal).
2 cups = 1 pint, 2 pints = 1 quart, 4 quarts = 1 gallon.
Benchmarks: a drinking cup holds about 1 cup, and a large water container holds several gallons. Palm oil and kerosene are often sold by the gallon.

To estimate, compare with a benchmark: this bag feels like about 4 bags of sugar, so about 8 pounds if each sugar bag is 2 pounds.

### Objectives

- Know the relationships between ounces and pounds, and between cups, pints, quarts and gallons.
- Estimate mass and capacity using benchmarks.

### Classwork

- Lift and compare: learners hold two objects and predict which is heavier, then check with a balance or scale.
- Pouring station: find how many cups fill a pint, quart and gallon container using water or sand.
- Market survey: list items sold by the pound or by the gallon.

### Practice

1. How many ounces are in 2 pounds? **Answer:** 32 ounces
2. How many cups are in 1 quart? **Answer:** 4 cups
3. Which is heavier: a 50-pound bag of rice or 40 pounds of cassava? **Answer:** The 50-pound bag of rice
4. Is a bucket's capacity about 3 cups or 3 gallons? **Answer:** 3 gallons

### Homework

1. How many quarts are in 2 gallons? **Answer:** 8 quarts
2. How many pints are in 3 quarts? **Answer:** 6 pints
3. Name one item at home that weighs about 1 pound. **Answer:** Answers vary (for example, a bag of sugar or a tin of milk)

### Quiz

1. About how much does a pencil weigh? (1 ounce or less / 1 pound / 10 pounds / 1 gallon) **Answer:** 1 ounce or less
2. How many pints are in 1 gallon? (8 / 4 / 2 / 16) **Answer:** 8
3. Which unit would measure the water in a large drum? (Gallons / Ounces / Cups / Inches) **Answer:** Gallons

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

### Exit assessment

A gallon of palm oil is poured into quart bottles. How many bottles are filled? (4 / 2 / 8 / 16) **Answer:** 4

### Teacher notes

Use water or dry sand only, and wipe spills to prevent slipping. Record estimate-then-check results as teacher-observation evidence.

### Explanation

Metric units are linked by multiples of 10, 100 and 1,000:
10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m = 1 km
1,000 g = 1 kg

Converting from a larger unit to a smaller unit: multiply, because you need more of the smaller units.
3 m = 3 x 100 = 300 cm. 4 kg = 4 x 1,000 = 4,000 g.

Converting from a smaller unit to a larger unit: divide, because you need fewer of the larger units.
500 cm = 500 / 100 = 5 m. 6,000 g = 6,000 / 1,000 = 6 kg.

Mixed units: 2 m 45 cm = 200 + 45 = 245 cm. 3,250 g = 3 kg 250 g.

Check that the answer makes sense: there should be more centimetres than metres for the same length.

### Objectives

- Convert between mm, cm, m and km.
- Convert between g and kg.
- Write measurements in mixed units.

### Classwork

- Conversion chart: learners build a chart linking mm, cm, m and km with arrows marked x and /.
- Metre stick: count 100 cm in 1 m and 10 mm in 1 cm.
- Market bags (example weights): convert bag weights from kg to g.

### Practice

1. 5 m = ___ cm **Answer:** 500
2. 7 cm = ___ mm **Answer:** 70
3. 3,000 m = ___ km **Answer:** 3
4. 2 kg 400 g = ___ g **Answer:** 2,400

### Homework

2. 450 cm = ___ m ___ cm **Answer:** 4 m 50 cm
3. A road is 6 km long. How many metres is that? **Answer:** 6,000 m

### Quiz

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

| Example-data labels | (example weights) |

**Known uncertainties:** 
- MOE extraction confidence MEDIUM (CONTINUATION_PAGE_ASSIGNMENT).

### Explanation

To add or subtract measurements, first make sure they are in the same units.

Adding: 2 m 65 cm + 1 m 50 cm.
Add metres: 2 + 1 = 3 m. Add centimetres: 65 + 50 = 115 cm = 1 m 15 cm.
Total: 3 m + 1 m 15 cm = 4 m 15 cm.

Subtracting: 5 kg 200 g - 2 kg 700 g.
The grams cannot be taken directly (200 < 700), so regroup 1 kg as 1,000 g: 4 kg 1,200 g - 2 kg 700 g = 2 kg 500 g.

Another way: change everything to the smaller unit. 5,200 g - 2,700 g = 2,500 g = 2 kg 500 g.

Example: a tailor has 4 m of cloth and cuts 1 m 35 cm for a shirt. 400 cm - 135 cm = 265 cm = 2 m 65 cm is left.

### Objectives

- Add and subtract lengths in mixed metric units with regrouping.
- Add and subtract masses in kg and g with regrouping.

### Classwork

- Ribbon joining: join two measured pieces of string and measure the total to check the calculation.
- Market scale (example weights): add the weights of two bags and subtract a portion sold.
- Two methods: groups solve the same problem in mixed units and in the smaller unit only, then compare.

### Practice

1. 3 m 40 cm + 2 m 35 cm = ? **Answer:** 5 m 75 cm
2. 1 m 80 cm + 2 m 45 cm = ? **Answer:** 4 m 25 cm
3. 6 kg 300 g - 2 kg 800 g = ? **Answer:** 3 kg 500 g
4. A rope is 10 m long. 3 m 25 cm is cut off. How much is left? **Answer:** 6 m 75 cm

### Homework

1. 4 kg 650 g + 3 kg 500 g = ? **Answer:** 8 kg 150 g
2. 7 m 10 cm - 3 m 60 cm = ? **Answer:** 3 m 50 cm
3. A trader has a 25 kg bag of rice and sells 8 kg 500 g. How much is left? **Answer:** 16 kg 500 g

### Quiz

### Explanation

Perimeter is the distance all the way around a shape. Add the lengths of all the sides.
A rectangle 6 m long and 4 m wide has perimeter 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 m. Short cut: 2 x (length + width) = 2 x (6 + 4) = 20 m.
A square with sides of 5 cm has perimeter 4 x 5 = 20 cm.

Area is the amount of flat space inside a shape, measured in square units (square centimetres, cm², or square metres, m²).
On squared paper, count the unit squares. A rectangle 6 squares long and 4 squares wide has 6 x 4 = 24 squares.
Area of a rectangle = length x width. Area of a square = side x side.

Perimeter and area are different: a 6 m by 4 m garden needs 20 m of fence (perimeter) and has 24 m² of ground to plant (area).

Two shapes can have the same perimeter and different areas: a 5 by 5 square and a 7 by 3 rectangle both have perimeter 20, but their areas are 25 and 21.

### Objectives

- Find the perimeter of squares and rectangles.
- Find the area of squares and rectangles by counting squares and by multiplying.
- Explain the difference between perimeter and area.

### Classwork

- Squared paper: draw rectangles with area 12 squares and compare their perimeters.
- School garden: measure a garden bed and calculate the fence needed and the planting area.
- Chalk grid: walk around a chalk rectangle (perimeter) and fill it with paper squares (area).

### Practice

1. Find the perimeter of a rectangle 8 cm by 3 cm. **Answer:** 22 cm
2. Find the area of a rectangle 8 cm by 3 cm. **Answer:** 24 cm²
3. Find the perimeter and area of a square with 6 m sides. **Answer:** Perimeter 24 m, area 36 m²
4. A rectangle has area 40 m² and length 8 m. What is its width? **Answer:** 5 m

### Homework

1. Find the perimeter of a rectangular room 5 m by 4 m. **Answer:** 18 m
2. Find the area of the same room. **Answer:** 20 m²
3. A square garden has perimeter 32 m. How long is each side? **Answer:** 8 m

### Quiz

1. What is the area of a rectangle 7 m by 5 m? (35 m² / 24 m / 12 m² / 35 m) **Answer:** 35 m²
2. What is the perimeter of a square with 9 cm sides? (36 cm / 81 cm / 18 cm / 81 cm²) **Answer:** 36 cm
3. Which tells how much fence is needed around a field? (Perimeter / Area / Volume / Mass) **Answer:** Perimeter

### Diagnostic check (before the lesson)

### Exit assessment
